Lineage and responsibility

What I was taught—and what I can honestly claim.

I practise a karate system I call Ryujin-te. The name is mine: the system came to me without a style name attached, and I chose one rather than invent a historical title it never had.

My teacher told me that he learned the system in occupied Okinawa in the mid-1940s from an elderly local master. Over many years, he taught me the complete practice—empty-hand forms, weapons forms and a separate sword curriculum. He privately and verbally conferred fifth dan on me and entrusted me with preserving what I had received.

No certificate exists, and no outside organization has verified the rank or lineage. I state that plainly because I would rather show honest work than sell a legend.

Historical verdict

Plausible.
Well-evidenced.
Unproven. That is the evidentiary ceiling, not a temporary marketing disclaimer.

The tradition carries an origin account reaching back before 1945. I hold it as the lineage's testimony, not as documented fact. Some comparative findings have held up against outside sources; others have not. Where the evidence fails, the claim stops.

As far as the transmitted lines currently available to me show, I am the last known active practitioner. Another possible line remains unaccounted for, so I do not claim that no other practitioner can exist.

The research

The record matters as much as the story.

Ryujin-te is being documented so the system can be examined, corrected, taught and preserved beyond one person's memory.

  1. 01

    Preserve the baseline

    Written and video records separate what was transmitted from conclusions reached later.

  2. 02

    Test before declaring

    Comparisons against Chinese and Okinawan sources follow stated criteria rather than resemblance alone.

  3. 03

    Record corrections

    Hypotheses that fail are abandoned and logged. A visible correction is stronger than a spotless legend.

  4. 04

    Publish responsibly

    Selected findings will be released without surrendering private student material or unpublished technical content.

Common questions

Ask the difficult questions.

Ryujin-te does not depend on you suspending judgment.

Is Ryujin-te an ancient historical style?

No. Ryujin-te is a modern name for a privately transmitted system. Its post-war Okinawan account comes through Philip's teacher; the earlier origin account remains oral and unproven.

How can the rank be legitimate without a certificate?

Philip's fifth dan and responsibility for the system were privately and verbally conferred by his now-deceased teacher. They are lineage claims, not association credentials. Prospective students are entitled to know that distinction.

Why don't beginners spar?

Contact requires control. Beginners first build movement, technique, kata and judgment. Partner training is introduced later according to demonstrated readiness—not merely time served.

Do I need a gi?

No. The received tradition did not use one, and the founding program uses comfortable athletic clothing. If a uniform or belt convention is adopted later, it will be identified honestly as a modern teaching choice.

Do I need to be fit or experienced?

No previous martial-arts experience is required. Instruction begins from the student's actual capabilities. Anyone with health concerns should consult an appropriate health professional before beginning physical training.

Can I see the full curriculum or research?

The curriculum progression can be explained to students, while unpublished technical and sword material remains reserved. Selected historical findings and corrections will be published progressively.
