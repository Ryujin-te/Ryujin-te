What you will practise

Technique first, then forms, then years of refinement.

You will spend a long time on a small number of things until your body does them correctly without being told. The fighting content is real. It is introduced only when you have the control for it.

It can be as demanding as you want it to be. Say so, and I will push you.

01

Learn how to move

We begin with standing, shifting weight, stepping and striking. Much of the first month does not look like karate. It is the body method everything else requires.

02

Learn the forms

The forms hold the system together. Each records a set of principles, gives you something exact to practise, and changes meaning as the rest of the curriculum catches up.

03

Keep going

Partner work, grappling, weapons and sparring come later. Behind the beginning is a complete ten-kyu and five-dan curriculum. You will not run out of material.

Your instructor

I am Philip.

I have trained in karate for about twenty years and hold fifth dan in Ryujin-te. It is the system my teacher handed on to me, and as far as I know I am the only person still practising it.

I have taught people from four years old into their nineties. For now, Ryujin-te instruction is for adults and is taught one to one.

I kept the standard my teacher gave me and discarded the methods that did not belong in a safe modern practice.

Training safely

Control comes before contact.

You work on movement, technique and forms first. Partner work, sparring and weapons arrive when you are ready, not when a fixed number of weeks has passed.

If something hurts, we stop. That rule is not negotiable.

Common questions

What new students usually ask.

Am I too old for this?

No. The training is adapted to what your body can do now. Recovery and mobility change with age; precision, timing and understanding can continue improving for decades. If you have a health condition, speak with your doctor first.

Will I get hurt?

Any physical activity carries risk, but beginners are not hitting people or being hit. Early training focuses on your own movement and control. If something hurts, we stop.

Will it work?

There is real fighting material in this system. I will not promise instant self-defence. Skill comes from sound mechanics, repetition and, eventually, carefully testing the material with a controlled partner.

What happens in the first lesson?

We discuss what you want and anything relevant about your body. Then I watch how you stand and move, introduce several fundamentals, and give you something precise to practise.

What does it cost?

$140 for a ninety-minute lesson, or $500 for four. The first conversation is free. There are no contracts, registration charges or grading fees.

Do I need a gi?

No. The tradition was not taught to me in a gi. Comfortable athletic clothing is sufficient.

Why do beginners not spar?

Because contact requires control, and control takes time. Partner work begins when you can work safely.

Where does the system come from?

The verifiable transmission begins in Okinawa after the Second World War. Before that is a family legend involving a fisherman, a demon and the Dragon God of the Sea. The complete account is here.
