What you will practise

A technical, form-centred karate with a long road ahead.

Ryujin-te is built around individual technique and kata. Beginners repeat the fundamentals until movement becomes coordinated, controlled and useful. There is real combative content here, but nobody is thrown into contact before they can control themselves.

Lessons can include conditioning and striking fundamentals alongside the traditional curriculum, depending on what you want from your training.

01

Learn how to move

Early lessons develop stance, balance, alignment, stepping and striking mechanics. You learn what each movement asks of your body, not merely where to put your hands.

02

Build a practice

Technique leads into kata: structured forms that preserve the system and give you something substantial to practise between lessons. Progress comes from attention, repetition and correction.

03

Earn greater depth

Partner work, sparring, weapons and sword work come later, for students who develop the necessary control. A ten-kyu and five-dan curriculum provides years of material to explore.

Your instructor

I'm Philip Piluris.

I've spent twenty years in karate and hold fifth dan in Ryujin-te. I received the complete system from my teacher, along with the responsibility to keep it alive. I've taught individually for years, from young children to people in their nineties.

Ryujin-te is built for adults who want to train seriously—people returning to martial arts after twenty years away, and people starting properly for the first time in their forties or fifties. Younger adults are welcome; this simply isn't a children's program.

I'm comfortable teaching complete beginners. You don't need to arrive fit, flexible or already fluent in martial arts. You need curiosity, patience and a willingness to practise.

The history is unusual, but I won't ask you to take it on faith. I'd rather show you the karate, explain what I know, and be equally clear about what remains uncertain.

Common questions

The things a new student actually needs to know.

Am I too old, unfit or inexperienced?

No previous martial-arts experience is required, and instruction begins from your actual capabilities. I have taught people across an unusually wide age range. Training can be adapted, although anyone with health concerns should consult an appropriate health professional first.

Will I get hurt?

All physical training carries some risk, but beginners do not spar or make contact with one another. The early curriculum emphasizes controlled individual movement. You are expected to communicate limitations and stop when something feels unsafe.

Will this actually work?

Ryujin-te contains real combative material, but I do not promise instant self-defence or sell confidence by slogan. Useful skill comes from sound mechanics, repeated practice, judgment and eventually testing material with appropriate partners.

What happens in the first private lesson?

We discuss your goals and relevant limitations, look at how you stand and move, introduce a small number of fundamentals, and finish with a manageable practice plan. Wear comfortable athletic clothing and bring water.

Do I need a gi?

No. The received tradition did not use one, and beginners train in comfortable athletic clothing. If I adopt uniforms or belt conventions later, I will say plainly that they are modern teaching choices, not something handed down.

Why don't beginners spar?

Contact requires control. Beginners first build movement, technique, kata and judgment. Partner training comes later according to demonstrated readiness, not merely time served.

What is the lineage?

Ryujin-te is the modern name I gave a privately transmitted system learned by my teacher in occupied Okinawa in the mid-1940s. My fifth dan and responsibility for the system were conferred privately and verbally. Read the complete lineage and research account.

What does it cost?

A ninety-minute private lesson is $140, or $500 for four. The introductory conversation is free. There are no contracts, registration fees, uniform costs or grading fees.

How often should I train?

Most students train weekly or fortnightly and practise between lessons. Kata rewards repetition on your own time—the lesson is for correction and new material, not for supervised drilling.

Where do lessons happen?

Within about thirty minutes of Vaughan City Hall, including Maple, Woodbridge, Concord, Thornhill and Richmond Hill. Lessons can take place at your home, outdoors in good weather, or at a booked space.
