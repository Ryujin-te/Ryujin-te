The training path

What you would actually learn

Most schools can show you eighteen months of syllabus. This one has twenty years of it, and it was finished long before I was born.

That cuts both ways, so let me be clear at the start. There is a complete path here, from a first lesson to the end of what I know. There is also no clock on it, and no one is going to tell you that you are behind.

The shape of it

A system large enough for a lifetime.

≈30

Empty-hand forms

These are the spine. Each is a compressed record of a set of principles, and the system is built so they teach one another. What you learn in the second form explains something in the ninth.

15

Weapons forms

Across the bo, sai, tonfa, eku, kama, and the rochin and tinbe. They are not a separate discipline bolted on. They sit inside the grading structure because weapons work teaches things about distance and body mechanics that the empty-hand forms assume you already understand.

10 + 5

Kyu and dan grades

Ten kyu grades and five dan grades, with specific content attached to each. Two forms per kyu grade. Three per dan grade, drawn from the remaining empty-hand material and the weapons curriculum.

Sword material, past all of it. Most students will never see this, and it is not a loss. It exists because it was given to me and it has to go somewhere.

Roughly twenty forms to reach black belt. Fifteen years of material after it.

The road

Progress measured in capability, not attendance.

  1. 01

    The first month

    Standing, moving, shifting weight, and striking properly. Almost none of it looks like karate. Most people feel they are learning nothing, and then one day the body does something correctly without being asked.

  2. 02

    The first year

    Two or three forms, done badly and improving. Real striking mechanics. The beginnings of the body method that runs underneath everything else. You will hold a stance under pressure and move out of it without falling, which sounds small and is not.

  3. 03

    Three years

    Eight or nine forms. Partner work, introduced carefully. The system begins to talk to itself, and forms you learned in the first year start meaning something different.

  4. 04

    Five years

    Somewhere near black belt, depending entirely on you. Weapons work begins. At this point you are no longer learning karate. You are doing karate and refining it.

  5. 05

    Beyond

    More forms, deeper applications, and the material that only makes sense once the body carries twenty forms already. This is the part that does not fit on a flyer, and it is the part that matters.

If you already train

You will not be asked to pretend you are a beginner.

Some of what follows will be of more interest to you than to a beginner.

Around thirty forms you have never seen

If you hold rank in a mainstream style, you have likely met every kata your system contains, and some of you have been repeating them for twenty years. This curriculum is not in any public syllabus. There is no video of it, no manual, and no one else teaching it. Whatever else it is, it is not more of what you already have.

A different body method

The postural rules here differ from the mainstream Okinawan lineages on specific, identifiable points. If you have trained hard in another system, this is the part that will take you longest, because you will be unlearning rather than learning. Experienced students move quickly through the fundamentals and slowly through this. That is normal and it is worth the discomfort.

Grappling, throwing and close work

The system did not come out of a sport context and it never dropped the material that sport rules remove. If you have a wrestling or judo background you will recognise more here than you expect, and you will find it arriving from angles you do not.

Rank does not transfer, and you will not start from nothing

I cannot grade you in a system you have not learned. But someone with twenty years of training does not begin where a beginner begins, and I am not going to pretend otherwise or make you serve time to prove a point. You will be taught at the level you can actually work at.

On competition, honestly

This is not a sport system. There is no tournament lineage, no point-fighting adaptation, and nothing here shaped by a rulebook. If your goal is a podium, another school will serve you better and I will say so on the phone rather than take your money.

What does transfer to a competitor is the mechanics: structure under load, timing, distance, and power generated from the ground rather than the shoulder. Those are style-agnostic and this system is unusually detailed about them.

On difficulty

Nothing on this website will have suggested that this is hard, because most of the people reading it are worried about whether they can start at all. So, plainly: it can be as demanding as you want it to be. The conditioning is real, the deep forms are long and exacting, and the partner work at the far end is not gentle. I was taught by a man who trained me harder than was reasonable, and I have kept the standard while discarding his methods. If you want to be pushed, say so, and I will push you.

About grading

Rank reflects capability.

01

Grading is optional

If you want to be graded, you will be. If you would rather train and never test for anything, that is a legitimate way to do this, and the material is the same either way.

02

There are no grading fees

Not reduced fees. None. You are not buying a rank and I am not selling one.

03

There is no schedule

You will not be tested because six months have passed. You will be tested when you can do the thing, however long that takes.

04

Rank reflects capability

Not attendance, not loyalty, not time served.

Starting late

The forms do not ask you to be fast. They ask you to be exact.

You will not progress the way a twenty-year-old does, and anyone who tells you otherwise is selling something. Recovery is slower and joints have opinions. That is real.

What is also real is that this system was built by men who trained into their eighties and nineties. Bushi was in his seventies when he fought two soldiers half his age and beat them both at once. Temur was past ninety the last time he came at me seriously, and he still read my intentions before I finished forming them. Neither was working on athleticism. They were working on precision, timing, structure, and sixty years of understanding what a body does under pressure.

Those things do not decline with age. They are the only things in martial arts that reliably improve with it.

The forms do not ask you to be flexible or fast. They ask you to be exact. Begin at fifty, train intelligently for fifteen years, and you will be sixty-five and genuinely dangerous, and you will understand things most twenty-five-year-old black belts do not.

What this is for

The punching is how it gets installed.

The system carries an ethic, and it is not decoration. It comes down to doing the right thing, in the right way, and it appears in the history of this lineage in a particular form. Every man in this line did something wrong, recognised it, and made amends.

Bushi spent months drunk and provoking armed men, looking for a way out. Two soldiers tried to stop him and he attacked them for it. Then he sobered up, met them honourably, and gave them everything he had.

Temur taught me harder than he needed to. At the end he handed the whole system over without reservation and removed himself so I would not stand in his shadow.

I met him because I was being beaten and doing nothing about it. He asked me why. I said I was afraid of hurting someone.

Being afraid is not the problem. Everyone is afraid. The problem is what fear does when you let it decide, which is that it takes your voice, and then it keeps taking.

What this training gives you, if you stay with it, is the confidence to do the right thing whatever it costs. Stand up to the bully. Say the unpopular thing when the room does not want it. Object to the injustice in front of you rather than the comfortable one at a distance.

I cannot separate this from the rest of my life and have stopped trying. It is in how I work, how I speak to people, and what I am willing to tolerate. That is the real thing on offer. The punching is how it gets installed.

Where to start

Twenty minutes. No obligation. No physical activity.

Ask whatever you like, including whether this is reasonable at your age or with your background. You will get an honest answer.
